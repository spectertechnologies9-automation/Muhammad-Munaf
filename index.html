About

A life of service and letters

Muhammad Munaf — also known as M.D. Munaf — was born into a family of modest means in Parashurampura, a village near Challakere in Chitradurga district, and completed his schooling in the village. As a high-school student he dreamed of serving the nation as a soldier. That door did not open, but his working life would take him to India's frontiers all the same, in service of the armed forces.

Moving to Bengaluru in search of work, he joined BEML Limited — the Government of India enterprise under the Ministry of Defence that builds heavy defence equipment and metro trains. Over 34 years he rose to senior superintendent in its Defence Business and Service division, retiring on 30 June 2017.

Alongside his career he kept alive a love of literature that began in childhood. Through poetry, prose, theatre, literary conferences and Kannada movements, he has served the language and culture of Karnataka for two decades — writing books of travel, biography and verse, and building places where others could meet Kannada literature.

Senior figures of Kannada literature — among them Nadoja Prof. K.S. Nisar Ahmed, Padma Shri Dr. Doddarangegowda, Nadoja Dr. Siddalingaiah and Prof. Hi.Chi. Boralingaiah — have written in appreciation of his work.

Professional Journey

Thirty-four years in service of India's defence

At BEML, Munaf's work centred on BEML-built Tatra vehicles — from 4×4 to 12×12 variants, including the field artillery trucks that tow Bofors guns. As a proof operator and service specialist he took part in production testing and trial evaluation, resolved issues raised by Army units in the field, and trained personnel to operate and maintain the equipment. He served as a civilian technical specialist with a defence public-sector company, not as a member of the armed forces.

The high Himalaya

Training and repair camps for Army personnel at altitudes of 14,000–19,000 ft, in temperatures as low as −18 °C — in the Siachen region, Khardung La, Leh–Ladakh, the Nubra and Hunder valleys, Kargil, Sikkim, and at Sela Top and Jaswantgarh in Arunachal Pradesh.

The Rajasthan desert

Vehicle trials and training camps in extreme desert heat at Army stations in Jodhpur, Jaisalmer, Pokhran, Bikaner, Nasirabad, Ganganagar and Surajgarh.

The islands and beyond

Service visits to defence stations in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, including the Air Force Station at Car Nicobar, and repair and technical training for radar equipment, including at Kudankulam in Tamil Nadu.

A moment near Sela Top

In 2003, during a quality trial of a Bofors-towing Tatra truck in the snowbound Tawang district of Arunachal Pradesh, the Army driver lost control on a turn near Sela Top and the vehicle was about to fall. Munaf's quick response brought it under control, saving seven Army personnel, including a Major. BEML recorded the episode in an appreciation certificate issued on 15 August 2013.

Language, Culture & Society

Building places for Kannada

Literary conferences in Sir C.V. Raman Nagar

As president of the Kannada Sahitya Parishat unit for the Sir C.V. Raman Nagar assembly constituency (2016–20), he organised two district-level Kannada literary conferences in an area largely home to workers from outside the Kannada-speaking community. Guests included Nadoja Dr. Siddalingaiah, Padma Shri Dr. Doddarangegowda, Dr. L. Hanumanthaiah and former Chief Secretary Dr. Chiranjeevi Singh. In his term the unit held more than 27 literary and cultural programmes — the most among the 28 constituency units of the Bengaluru district Parishat.

Kannada in schools

Through the “Kannada Kalika” initiative he took programmes such as “Kannadada Angaladalli Tingala Belaku” and “Sahitya Jyoti” — talks, poetry readings and discussions — to more than 30 schools and colleges, and honoured talented teachers and students.

Two community libraries

At the request of senior citizens and residents, he set up two libraries with about 3,000 books in the constituency, including one at New Thippasandra. Both continue to serve readers.

A statue of Dr. Rajkumar

He led the installation of a statue of Dr. Rajkumar near the new Baiyappanahalli railway station in Bengaluru, unveiled in the presence of Kannada activist Sa.Ra. Govindu and producer Chinnegowda.

Kannada movements

He has taken part in movements on the Kalasa–Banduri water-sharing question, against Hindi imposition, for implementation of the Sarojini Mahishi report and for Kannada in administration, and in the Cauvery agitation.

Service in hard times

During the COVID-19 pandemic he worked with partner organisations to distribute food to hundreds of people every day. He has helped 57 contract workers of modest means obtain driving training and licences, and joined environmental and communal-harmony campaigns.

A voice for his community

As state divisional vice-president of the Karnataka Rajya Nadaf/Pinjara Sangha, he travels across the state working for the educational, social and economic progress of the Nadaf/Pinjara community.

On the stage

He has also performed in Kannada theatre, including the role of Arjuna in the play Sri Krishna Sandhana.
